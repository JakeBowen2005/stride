# Marathon Training Science & Workout Library

> Research-backed training guide for marathon plan generation.
> This is a SEPARATE research file — NOT the athlete-specific plan notes.
> Sources: Pfitzinger (Advanced Marathoning), Daniels, Canova, Hansons, Running Writings,
> Runners Connect, Frontiers in Sports, PubMed studies.

## Physiological Demands of the Marathon

- Marathon is raced at **~75% of VO2max** for trained runners
- Race pace sits **below lactate threshold** — but only barely for faster runners
- ~99% aerobic contribution
- Race duration: 2:30-5:00+ — long enough that **glycogen depletion** becomes the primary limiter
- The "wall" is real: **>40% of marathon runners** experience severe glycogen depletion (PubMed)
- Key limiters (in order): **Glycogen storage/utilization**, lactate threshold, VO2max, running economy, muscular endurance, physiological resilience

### The Four Pillars of Marathon Fitness (Running Writings / John Davis)
1. **VO2max** — the aerobic ceiling
2. **Running economy** — efficiency of movement (oxygen cost per pace)
3. **Lactate threshold** — highest sustainable pace before lactate accumulates
4. **Physiological resilience** — the ability to resist decline in fitness as the race continues
   - Muscle damage resistance
   - Glycogen preservation
   - Central fatigue resistance

### Why Physiological Resilience Matters
Research shows three mechanisms cause late-marathon fatigue:
1. **Muscle damage**: Eccentric contractions damage fibers, reducing force production. Running 30 min downhill can increase oxygen cost of running by 4-7%.
2. **Localized glycogen depletion**: Even when total glycogen remains, local depletion at calcium pump sites causes muscle failure.
3. **Central fatigue**: Motor cortex output to legs decreases by ~18% post-marathon (while grip strength stays unchanged).

**Training implication**: High mileage, long runs, and long fast runs build resilience against all three.

## Key Training Systems for Marathon

### 1. Mileage & Aerobic Base (The Foundation — #1 Priority)
- **Why**: Marathon is an aerobic event. Volume is the single greatest predictor of performance.
- **Pfitzinger**: "The most important thing a marathoner can do is run more miles"
- **Mileage builds**: capillary density, mitochondrial density, fat oxidation, glycogen storage capacity, running economy, muscular endurance
- **80/20 rule**: ~80% easy, ~20% quality
- **Easy pace**: Conversational. The emphasis is on effort, not a specific pace number.
- **Research**: Sub-3 typically requires 50-70 mpw peak. Sub-2:45 often requires 60-80+.

### 2. Lactate Threshold Development
- **Why**: Marathon pace sits just below LT for competitive runners. Raising LT raises MP.
- **Threshold work**: Tempo runs (20-40min continuous), cruise intervals (2-3mi reps)
- **Pfitzinger's approach**: Threshold first in periodization, then VO2max intervals closer to race
- **For marathon**: Threshold pace = 15K to half marathon race pace (~25-30 sec/mi faster than MP)
- **Progression**: 2x2mi → 3x2mi → 2x3mi → 4x2mi at threshold
- **Float recovery**: Moderate jog between reps (not full rest) — keeps HR elevated, simulates race

### 3. Marathon Pace (MP) Specificity
- **Why**: The marathon demands holding one specific pace for 2-4+ hours. Must be automatic.
- **MP runs**: 3-8mi continuous at exact goal pace
- **MP long runs**: The signature marathon workout. 10mi easy + 8mi at MP, etc.
- **Canova's approach**: Long fast runs at 87-95% of PR marathon pace, done every 2 weeks
- **Key principle**: Everything revolves around goal race pace. Workouts exist to support MP.
- **Progression**: MP runs extend from 3mi → 4mi → 5mi → 6mi over weeks. MP in long runs extends from 3mi → 5mi → 8mi → 10mi.

### 4. VO2max Development (The Ceiling)
- **Why**: Higher VO2max = more room between MP and max effort = more sustainable
- **How**: 800m-1mi reps at 5K pace, 1x per week or every 10-14 days
- **Daniels**: Interval pace work early in cycle, threshold shifts later
- **Pfitzinger**: Threshold early, VO2max intervals come later (closer to race day)
- **For marathon**: VO2max sessions are shorter and less frequent than for 5K/10K
- **Purpose**: Maintain the aerobic ceiling, don't let it shrink during high-volume training

### 5. Long Run Development (The Marathon-Specific Session)
- **Why**: The long run IS the marathon workout. It builds every system simultaneously.
- **Pfitzinger**: 10-16 runs of 16+ miles, 3-8 runs of 20+ miles in a cycle
- **Types**: Easy long run, progressive, steady finish, MP finish, sandwich, MP block
- **Canova long fast runs**: 20-40K at 87-95% PR marathon pace — elite-level endurance builder
- **Hansons**: Caps long runs at 16mi, relies on cumulative fatigue (running long on already tired legs)
- **Key principle**: Long runs should be SPECIFIC as the cycle progresses — not just easy miles

### 6. Running Economy & Speed Maintenance
- **Why**: Better economy = less oxygen cost at MP. But marathon runners don't need raw speed.
- **How**: Strides 2-3x/week (maintenance), occasional 400m reps, hill sprints in base phase
- **Minimal emphasis** compared to 5K/10K — economy gains come primarily from high mileage
- **Don't neglect entirely**: Strides keep fast-twitch fibers active during heavy aerobic training

## Periodization for Marathon (14-20 week cycle)

### Phase 1: Base / Endurance (4-6 weeks)
- Build mileage progressively
- All easy + strides (maybe 1 fartlek per week)
- Long runs at easy effort (12-16mi)
- Medium-long run midweek (10-13mi at easy pace)
- No structured workouts initially — let volume do the work
- Purpose: Aerobic foundation, injury-proof the body

### Phase 2: Build / Threshold Focus (4-6 weeks)
- Threshold becomes primary quality session (1-2x/week)
- Introduce MP runs (3-5mi continuous)
- Long runs start including quality: steady finish, progression
- VO2max intervals 1x per 10-14 days (Pfitzinger introduces these here)
- Continue building mileage toward peak
- Purpose: Raise LT, introduce race-pace specificity

### Phase 3: Specific / Peak (4-6 weeks)
- MP long runs become primary: 10+8 MP, 10+10 MP, sandwich, MP block
- Threshold maintained (1x/week, slightly reduced volume)
- MP runs extend (5-8mi continuous at goal pace)
- VO2max 1x per 10-14 days
- Mileage at or near peak
- **This is where the marathon is built** — these long runs are the most important workouts
- Purpose: Race simulation, glycogen training, muscular endurance at MP

### Phase 4: Taper (2-3 weeks)
- **Volume reduction: Progressive — 80% → 60% → 30-40% of peak**
- **Research (PMC 2021)**: Strict 3-week tapers associated with **5 min 32 sec (2.6%)** faster finishes
- **Maintain intensity** — short quality sessions at MP and faster
- **Maintain frequency** — don't cut days, shorten sessions (research confirms)
- **Do NOT reduce training frequency** — Bosquet meta-analysis: runners who kept their days saw better performance
- **Final quality session**: 5-6 days before race (short MP run or threshold intervals)

## Workout Library: Experienced Runners (50+ mpw)

> Paces: MP = marathon goal pace, TP = threshold pace (~25-30s/mi faster than MP),
> IP = 5K/VO2max pace, SP = strides/rep pace, STP = steady pace (~15-20s/mi faster than MP)

### 1. 3x2mi at TP (Staple Threshold)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog or 0.5mi float
- **Total hard**: 6mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: The bread-and-butter threshold workout. Raises LT — directly improves marathon pace capacity.
- **Progression**: 2x2mi → 3x2mi → 2x3mi → 4x2mi.
- **When**: All phases after base. The most important non-long-run workout.

### 2. 4x2mi at TP (Peak Threshold Volume)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog or 0.5mi float
- **Total hard**: 8mi at threshold — massive stimulus
- **Purpose**: Peak threshold volume. 8 miles at threshold builds the engine for marathon pace.
- **When**: Peak phase. Only prescribe at 55+ mpw.

### 3. 2x3mi at TP (Extended Threshold)
- **Rest**: 1mi easy float
- **Total hard**: 6mi in longer sustained chunks
- **Purpose**: Longer reps teach sustaining threshold effort for extended periods. Marathon-specific endurance.
- **When**: Build/peak phases.

### 4. MP Pace Run — 4mi
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace. Controlled — can push if feeling good but don't force it.
- **Purpose**: Practice holding race rhythm. Introduction to sustained MP work.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 5. MP Pace Run — 6mi
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace. This is a hard workout — stay disciplined.
- **Purpose**: Extended MP work. 6 miles at race pace builds massive confidence.
- **When**: Peak phase.

### 6. MP Pace Run — 8mi
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace. Mentally and physically demanding.
- **Purpose**: Peak MP session outside of the long run. 8 miles at race effort is a race simulation.
- **When**: Peak phase. Only for experienced runners at 60+ mpw.

### 7. 3x3mi at MP (Peak MP Volume)
- **Rest**: 0.5mi jog
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace
- **Total hard**: 9mi at MP — brutal
- **Purpose**: Peak marathon-specific volume workout. Only prescribe after athlete has proven they can hold MP for 4-5mi in workouts and long runs.
- **Requirement**: 50+ mpw, demonstrated MP readiness.
- **When**: Peak phase (final 4-6 weeks).

### 8. 4x1mi at IP (VO2max Session)
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 4mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Maintains/raises VO2max ceiling. Mile reps for marathon runners — long enough to build sustained speed.
- **When**: Build/peak phases. Every 10-14 days.

### 9. 6x800m at IP (VO2max)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Standard VO2max session. Maintains aerobic ceiling during heavy volume training.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 10. 5x1K at IP + 2mi at TP (Combo)
- **Rest**: 400m jog between 1Ks, 3min before threshold block
- **Total hard**: ~3mi VO2max + 2mi threshold
- **Purpose**: VO2max stimulus with threshold finish. Running threshold on VO2max-fatigued legs.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 11. 4x1.5mi at TP-10sec (Extended Reps)
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 6mi at near-threshold pace
- **Purpose**: An easier threshold day. Pace slightly faster than threshold but more reps. Think of it as threshold with extra volume, not interval pace.
- **When**: Build/peak phases. Good for weeks with a hard long run.

### 12. Threshold + MP Combo: 2x2mi at TP + 3mi at MP
- **Rest**: 2min between threshold reps, 3min before MP block
- **Total hard**: 7mi of quality (4mi threshold + 3mi MP)
- **Purpose**: Combines threshold and race-pace specificity. Running MP on threshold-fatigued legs simulates miles 18-22 of the marathon.
- **When**: Peak phase.

### 13. Canova Special Block (Adapted for Amateur)
- **Structure**: AM: 10mi with 6mi at 90-95% MP. PM: 6mi with 4x1K at 5K pace.
- **Total**: ~16mi quality in one day
- **Purpose**: Massive marathon-specific stimulus in concentrated form. Elite-level training adapted down.
- **When**: Peak phase. 1x per 3-4 weeks. Only for advanced runners at 60+ mpw.
- **Important**: Reduce volume the day before and 2 days after.

### 14. Canova Alternating K: 10x1K at 105% MP with 1K at MP Recovery
- **Rest**: 1K at marathon pace (continuous running — NOT a jog)
- **Total hard**: 20K total (10K slightly faster than MP + 10K at MP)
- **Purpose**: Canova's signature lactate oxidation workout. Running fast with fast recovery teaches the body to clear lactate while maintaining pace. The "recovery" K at MP means you're doing 20K of quality.
- **When**: Peak phase. Only for advanced runners at 55+ mpw.

## Long Run Library: Experienced Runners

> These are the long runs that BUILD marathon fitness. The long run is the marathon workout.

### Standard Long Runs (All Phases)

#### Easy Long Run (12-20mi)
- **Structure**: All miles at easy, conversational pace
- **Purpose**: Aerobic endurance, capillary density, fat-burning efficiency
- **When**: Base phase (all long runs), build/peak (alternating with quality long runs)

#### Steady Finish Long Run (14-18mi)
- **Structure**: 10-14mi easy, last 3-4mi at steady effort (~15-20s/mi faster than easy, ~20-30s/mi slower than MP)
- **Purpose**: Teaches finishing strong on tired legs. Controlled — not a workout.
- **When**: Build phase. Introduction to quality long runs.

#### Progressive Long Run (16-18mi)
- **Structure**: Start easy, each 3-4mi segment gets slightly faster, last 2-3mi at steady-to-threshold
- **Purpose**: Pacing discipline over distance. Negative-split mentality.
- **When**: Build phase.

### Marathon-Specific Long Runs (Peak Phase, 55+ mpw)

#### MP Finish Long Run (16-18mi)
- **Structure**: 12-14mi easy, 4-5mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: Race simulation on tired legs. The key marathon long run workout.
- **When**: Peak phase. Every 2-3 weeks.

#### 10 + 8 MP Long Run (18mi)
- **Structure**: 10mi easy, 8mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: Simulates the back half of a marathon on tired legs. Race day rehearsal.
- **When**: Peak phase. 55+ mpw.

#### 10 + 9 MP Long Run (19mi)
- **Structure**: 10mi easy, 9mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: Mental toughness builder. Stay disciplined the whole way.
- **When**: Peak phase. 58+ mpw.

#### 10 + 10 MP Long Run (20mi)
- **Structure**: 10mi easy, 10mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: The ultimate marathon long run. 20 miles with half at race pace. Peak confidence builder.
- **When**: Peak phase. 60+ mpw. Use sparingly — 1-2x in the entire cycle.

#### Steady to MP Long Run (20mi)
- **Structure**: 5mi easy, 5mi at steady, 10mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: Progressive build from easy → steady → race pace. Teaches pacing discipline over 20 miles.
- **When**: Peak phase. 60+ mpw.

#### Sandwich MP Long Run (19mi)
- **Structure**: 4mi easy, 4mi at steady, 4mi easy, 7mi at goal MP
- **Purpose**: Mixed effort long run. Steady block primes the legs for the MP finish.
- **When**: Peak phase. 55+ mpw.

#### MP Block Long Run (20mi)
- **Structure**: 5mi easy, 3x4mi at goal MP with 1mi float between, 1mi cooldown
- **Purpose**: Accumulates massive marathon pace volume. Peak long run workout. 12mi at MP.
- **When**: Peak phase. 55+ mpw. Only prescribe 1-2x in the cycle.

### Hansons-Style Long Run Approach (Alternative)
- Caps long runs at **16mi** instead of 20
- Relies on **cumulative fatigue**: running 16mi on tired legs (from 6 runs per week, 3 quality sessions)
- The theory: 16mi after 5 days of running feels like mile 16-26 of a marathon
- Your body is never fully recovered — simulating race conditions
- **Mileage still high**: 55-70 mpw, just distributed differently
- **Pros**: Less injury risk from single long runs, more sustainable
- **Cons**: Never practices the actual 20-mile effort, less proven at elite level

## Workout Library: Beginner/Intermediate Runners (25-50 mpw)

- **Purpose**: Foundation threshold workout. Simple and effective.

- **Rest**: None
- **Purpose**: Sustained threshold effort. Progress from 20min → 25min → 30min.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 3. 4x800m at IP (Intro VO2max)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 2mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Introduction to faster running. Maintains speed while building marathon base.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 4. MP Pace Run — 3mi
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace. Controlled.
- **Purpose**: Introduction to race pace. Learn what MP feels like.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 5. MP Pace Run — 4mi
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: Exact marathon goal pace. Stay controlled.
- **Purpose**: Extended race-pace practice.
- **When**: Build/peak phase.

### 6. Fartlek: 6x3min Hard / 2min Easy
- **Rest**: 2min easy jog
- **Pacing**: Hard segments at threshold effort
- **Total hard**: 18min quality
- **Purpose**: Unstructured threshold work. Less intimidating than track intervals.
- **When**: Base/build phase.

### 7. 3x1mi at TP (Threshold Miles)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: Mile-based threshold. Easy to pace on roads.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 8. 5x1K at IP (Standard VO2max)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog
- **Total hard**: ~3mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Standard VO2max session. Maintains aerobic ceiling.

### 9. 2x2mi at TP + 2mi at MP (Combo)
- **Rest**: 2min between threshold reps, 3min before MP block
- **Total hard**: 6mi quality
- **Purpose**: Combines threshold and MP. Introduction to running MP on tired legs.
- **When**: Peak phase.

### 10. Progressive Long Run: 14-16mi
- **Structure**: 10-12mi easy, last 3-4mi descending to steady pace
- **Purpose**: Teaches finishing strong. Controlled quality on tired legs.
- **When**: Build phase. Every 2-3 weeks.

### 11. Easy Long Run (14-18mi)
- **Structure**: All easy. Conversational pace the entire time.
- **Purpose**: Aerobic endurance. The most important beginner long run.
- **When**: All phases. Most long runs should be this.

### 12. MP Finish Long Run: 14-16mi
- **Structure**: 11-13mi easy, 3mi at MP
- **Purpose**: Introduction to race-pace long runs. Short MP block (3mi) is manageable.
- **When**: Peak phase.

## Pace Calculation Guidelines for Plan Engine

### From Goal Marathon Time
- **Race Pace (MP)**: goal_time / 26.2188 (sec/mi)
- **Interval Pace (IP)**: MP * 0.85 (~15% faster — approximates 5K/VO2max pace)
- **Threshold Pace (TP)**: MP * 0.93 (~7% faster — half marathon / 15K effort)
- **Easy Pace**: MP * 1.25 to MP * 1.45 (25-45% slower)
- **Steady Pace**: TP + 25sec (within ~1min of MP — not the midpoint of easy/threshold)
- **Strides Pace (SP)**: MP * 0.75 (~mile race effort)
- **Long Run Pace**: MP * 1.30 (easy, conversational)

### Current Fitness Progression (Same as Already Built)
- Threshold: Starts at easy * 0.77, blends toward (goal MP - 25sec) as mileage builds 30-60 mpw
- Interval: Starts at easy * 0.67, blends toward goal interval pace
- Steady: threshold + 25sec
- Goal race pace: Exact goal MP — used for MP runs and long run MP portions
- All paces rounded to nearest 5 seconds

## Marathon-Specific Training Concepts

### Cumulative Fatigue (Hansons)
- The idea that running on already-tired legs simulates late-marathon conditions
- High frequency (6 days/week) means you're never fully recovered
- A 16mi long run on tired legs might feel like miles 16-26
- Works best with high weekly mileage (55+) and consistent running
- **Criticism**: Limited scientific evidence specifically supporting this claim
- **Our approach**: Use cumulative fatigue naturally through high mileage, but also include 18-20mi long runs

### Canova's Long Fast Runs
- 20-40K at 87-95% of PR marathon pace, done every 2 weeks
- Elite athletes sustain near-race pace for massive distances
- **Adapted for amateurs**: 12-16mi with 6-10mi at 90-95% MP
- Purpose: Builds "physiological resilience" — resistance to late-race deterioration
- When combined with easy weeks, produces rapid marathon-specific adaptation

### Canova's Lactate Oxidation Workouts
- Fast reps with fast recovery (NOT full rest)
- Example: 10x1K at 105% MP with 1K at 100-101% MP recovery
- The "recovery" is itself at marathon pace — massive sustained quality
- Purpose: Teaches body to clear lactate while maintaining pace
- **For amateurs**: Adapt as 6x1K at threshold with 1K at steady pace

### Glycogen Training
- Long runs at MP deplete glycogen stores specifically
- The body adapts by storing MORE glycogen in response
- This is why MP long runs are essential — they're glycogen training
- Long easy runs also deplete glycogen but less specifically
- **Fueling practice**: Train the gut to absorb carbs during MP long runs

### The "10% Rule" vs. Headroom-Based Ramp
- Traditional: Never increase weekly mileage by >10%
- Our approach: Headroom-based — if runner has peaked at 70mpw before, they can ramp faster from 30mpw
- Ratio of current/peak determines aggressiveness: <50% = 20% ramp, 50-75% = 15%, >75% = 10%
- More intelligent than a flat percentage

### Deload Timing
- Advanced: First deload at block 4 (week 7-8), then every 3rd block (~6 weeks)
- Beginner/Intermediate: First deload at block 3 (week 5-6), then every 2nd block (~4 weeks)
- Push through initial adaptation first, then deload once in a groove
- Deload = 1 week only, 25% volume cut, 1 workout (lighter), long run can still include quality
