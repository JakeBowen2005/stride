# 5K Training Science & Workout Library

> Research-backed training guide for 5K plan generation.
> Sources: Daniels' Running Formula, Billat VO2max research, Canova specific endurance,
> Seiler polarized model, Frontiers in Sports (2024), Runners Connect, Running Writings.

## Physiological Demands of the 5K

- 5K race effort sits at **95-100% of VO2max** for trained amateur runners
- At sub-17 pace (5:28/mi / 3:24/km), metabolic cost is ~60-63 ml/kg/min O2
- Sub-17 likely requires VO2max of **65-70+ ml/kg/min**
- Sub-20 likely requires VO2max of **55-60 ml/kg/min**
- The 5K is an **aerobic event** — ~95% aerobic, ~5% anaerobic contribution
- The binding constraint is usually the aerobic ceiling itself, not pacing or strength
- Key limiters: VO2max, lactate threshold, speed endurance, running economy

## Key Training Systems for 5K

### 1. VO2max Development (The #1 Priority)
- **Why**: 5K is raced at or near VO2max — raising the ceiling directly improves performance
- **How**: Intervals at 95-100% of vVO2max (velocity at VO2max)
- **Optimal rep duration**: 2-5 minutes (meta-analysis of 51 studies, ~1,200 participants)
  - Sweet spot identified: **~140 seconds (2:20)** per rep
  - Takes ~2 minutes of sustained effort for O2 uptake to reach VO2max
  - 3-5 minute reps produce the most time above 90% VO2max
- **Optimal session volume**: 15-20 minutes of accumulated time at VO2max intensity
- **Rest ratio**: 1:1 active recovery (jog at 50% vVO2max)
- **Frequency**: 1-2x per week during build/peak phases
- **Research finding (Frontiers 2024)**: 4x3min at 95% vVO2max with 3min jog recovery produced 328 seconds above 90% VO2max — significantly more than 24x30s at 100% vVO2max (201 seconds). Longer intervals win.

### 2. Lactate Threshold / Critical Velocity
- **Why**: Raising LT allows a higher % of VO2max to be sustained
- **Critical Velocity (CV)**: Fastest pace sustainable for 30-40 minutes, sits between LT and VO2max
- **CV training improves both LT and VO2max simultaneously** (research-confirmed)
- **After 6 weeks of threshold training**, velocity at LT and 5K pace both increased significantly
- **Threshold pace for 5K runners**: approximately 10K-15K race pace, or ~25-30 sec/mi slower than 5K goal pace
- **Tempo runs**: 12-30 minutes continuous at threshold effort
- **Cruise intervals**: 5-15 min reps at threshold with 1-3 min rest

### 3. Speed Endurance (Extending Fast Pace Over Duration)
- **Why**: The gap between having speed (200m, 400m) and sustaining it for 3.1 miles
- **How**: Race-pace and slightly-faster-than-race-pace reps with controlled rest
- **Key principle**: Reduce rest periods as fitness builds, NOT increase pace
- **Canova approach**: Practice exact race pace in training, extend the volume at that pace

### 4. Running Economy
- **Why**: Better economy = less oxygen cost at the same pace = can sustain faster paces
- **How**: Strides (neuromuscular), hill sprints, fast 200-400m reps
- **Strides**: 4-6x20sec at ~mile pace effort, 2-3x/week on easy days
- **200-400m reps**: Full recovery, focus on form and turnover

### 5. Aerobic Base (The Foundation)
- **Why**: Without volume, nothing else works. Mileage is the foundation of VO2max
- **How**: Easy miles at conversational pace, long runs
- **80/20 rule**: ~80% of mileage should be easy, ~20% quality
- **Long runs for 5K**: 8-12 miles, run easy (60-90 sec/mi slower than goal pace)

## Periodization for 5K (8-12 week cycle)

### Phase 1: Base (2-4 weeks)
- Build mileage, all easy + strides
- Long runs at easy effort
- No structured workouts yet (maybe 1 fartlek)
- Purpose: Aerobic foundation, injury-proof the body

### Phase 2: Build (3-4 weeks)
- Introduce threshold work (1x/week)
- Introduce VO2max intervals (1x/week)
- Continue building mileage
- Long runs can add pickups or progressive finish
- Purpose: Develop LT and VO2max simultaneously

### Phase 3: Peak / Specific (2-3 weeks)
- Race-specific workouts at goal 5K pace
- VO2max sessions shift toward 5K-specific reps
- Threshold work maintained but not increased
- Mileage holds or slightly decreases
- Purpose: Sharpen race fitness, practice goal pace

### Phase 4: Taper (7-10 days)
- **Volume reduction: 40-50%** of peak mileage
- **Maintain intensity** — reduce volume of hard sessions, not effort
- Cut easy run distances, keep 1-2 short speed sessions
- Final speed session 3-4 days before race
- **Research**: 8-14 day taper optimal for endurance; 5K evidence clusters at 7-10 days

#### Sample 5K Taper Week
| Day | Workout |
|-----|---------|
| Day -7 | Easy 3mi |
| Day -6 | 2mi WU, 6x400m at 5K pace, 1mi CD |
| Day -5 | Easy 3mi |
| Day -4 | Rest or 2mi very easy |
| Day -3 | 1.5mi WU, 4x600m at 5K pace, 1mi CD |
| Day -2 | Easy 2mi |
| Day -1 | 15min easy jog + 3-4 strides, or full rest |

## Workout Library: Experienced Runners (35+ mpw)

> Paces reference: RP = 5K race pace, TP = threshold pace (~25-30s/mi slower than RP),
> IP = interval pace (~5-10s/mi faster than RP), SP = strides/rep pace (~mile effort)

### 1. 6x1000m at RP (Classic 5K Workout)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog
- **Total hard**: 6K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Race-pace volume. Teaches the body exactly what 5K effort feels like.
- **Progression**: Start with 5x1K, build to 6x1K, then tighten rest from 2min to 90sec.
- **When**: Build and peak phases. The bread-and-butter 5K workout.

### 2. 5x1200m at RP
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 6K at race pace but in longer chunks
- **Purpose**: Extends time at race pace per rep. Builds sustained speed endurance.
- **When**: Peak phase. Harder than 6x1K because each rep is longer.

### 3. 4x1mi at RP to RP-5sec
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 4 miles at race pace
- **Purpose**: Mile repeats at 5K pace — the ultimate 5K confidence builder. If you can hold 5K pace for 4 separate miles, you can hold it for 3.1.
- **When**: Peak phase (2-3 weeks before race).

### 4. 8x800m at IP (VO2max Session)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog (1:1 ratio)
- **Total hard**: 4 miles at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Maximizes time above 90% VO2max. Research shows 3-5 min reps optimal — 800m at these paces is ~2:30-3:00.
- **When**: Build phase primarily. Daniels' staple I-pace workout.

### 5. 5x3min at 95-100% vVO2max (Billat Protocol)
- **Rest**: 3min jog at 50% vVO2max
- **Total hard**: 15min at VO2max intensity
- **Purpose**: Research-optimized VO2max stimulus. Produces ~328 sec above 90% VO2max per session. The most scientifically efficient VO2max workout.
- **When**: Build phase. 1-2x per month.

### 6. 3x2mi at TP (Threshold Session)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog
- **Total hard**: 6mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: Raises lactate threshold. More volume than tempo runs with manageable chunks.
- **When**: Build and peak phases. The workhorse threshold workout for experienced runners.

### 7. Canova 5K Specific: 5x(1K + 400m)
- **Rest**: 1min between 1K and 400m, 400m jog between sets (~3min)
- **Pacing**: 1K at 5K goal pace, 400m slightly faster than 5K pace
- **Total hard**: 5K at RP + 2K slightly faster
- **Purpose**: Race-specific with a speed kicker. Teaches running fast on tired legs.
- **When**: Peak phase. Canova's go-to 5K-specific session.

### 8. Canova Descending Ladder: 2K + 1600m + 1200m + 800m + 4x400m
- **Rest**: 200m jog between all reps
- **Pacing**: Start at 10K pace, progressively increase to 5K pace by 800m, 400s at 5K pace or faster
- **Total hard**: ~7.5K of quality
- **Purpose**: Teaches pace progression and finishing fast. Simulates the race-day acceleration.
- **When**: Build/peak transition.

### 9. 20x400m at RP with 200m jog (Supra-Threshold)
- **Rest**: 200m jog (~60-75sec)
- **Total hard**: 8K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Massive race-pace volume with short rest. Lactate stabilizes between reps while maintaining exact goal pace. Extremely race-specific.
- **When**: Peak phase. Only for experienced runners who can handle the volume.

### 10. 30/30s Billat (VO2max Maintenance)
- **Structure**: 16-20 reps of 30sec at vVO2max / 30sec at 50% vVO2max
- **Total hard**: 8-10min at VO2max
- **Purpose**: Accumulates VO2max time with less perceived effort. Billat's research shows O2 consumption stays at VO2max for 15-20sec even during the recovery, so you spend 45-50sec at VO2max per cycle.
- **When**: Build phase. 2-3x per month. Great mental break from traditional intervals.

### 11. Progression Long Run (Experienced 5K Runners)
- **Structure**: 8-10mi total. First 5-6mi easy, last 2-3mi descending to threshold pace, final mile at 10K effort
- **Purpose**: Builds aerobic endurance with a quality finish. Teaches pace discipline over distance.
- **When**: Every 2-3 weeks during build phase.

### 12. Race-Pace Tempo: 2mi at TP + 1mi at RP + 1mi at TP
- **Rest**: 2min jog between segments
- **Purpose**: Combines threshold and race pace in one session. The RP mile in the middle teaches what goal pace feels like on threshold-fatigued legs.
- **When**: Peak phase.

## Workout Library: Beginner/Intermediate Runners (15-35 mpw)

> Focus: Build aerobic base, introduce quality gradually, don't overdo intensity.
> Beginners should do 1 quality session per week for the first 4 weeks, then 2.

### 1. 4x800m at IP (Intro VO2max)
- **Rest**: 2min jog (generous recovery)
- **Total hard**: 2mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Introduction to VO2max training. Short enough to maintain form. Generous rest allows quality.
- **When**: Build phase. First interval workout for newer runners.

### 2. 6x400m at RP (Speed Introduction)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog
- **Total hard**: 1.5mi at race pace
- **Purpose**: Learn what 5K pace feels like in small doses. Low injury risk due to short reps.
- **When**: Build phase onwards. Can do this workout from week 1 of quality.

### 3. 2x10min at TP (Intro Threshold)
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 20min at threshold
- **Purpose**: Introduction to sustained hard effort. Builds lactate clearance.
- **When**: Build phase. The simplest threshold workout.

### 4. 3x1K at RP (Race Pace Practice)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 3K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Practice holding goal 5K pace for 1K at a time. Confidence builder.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 5. 20min Continuous Tempo
- **Rest**: None (continuous effort)
- **Total hard**: 20min at threshold
- **Purpose**: Teaches sustained effort without the crutch of rest intervals. Mental toughness.
- **When**: Build phase. Progress to 25-30min as fitness improves.

### 6. Fartlek: 8x1min Fast / 1min Easy
- **Rest**: Equal time easy jog
- **Pacing**: Fast segments at 5K effort (not pace — effort)
- **Total hard**: 8min at quality effort
- **Purpose**: Unstructured speed work. Lower stress than track intervals. Good for runners intimidated by the track.
- **When**: Early build phase. Transition workout before structured intervals.

### 7. Pyramid: 1-2-3-2-1 min Fast with 1min Easy
- **Rest**: 1min easy jog between all segments
- **Pacing**: Effort-based — "comfortably hard" to "hard"
- **Total hard**: 9min of quality
- **Purpose**: Teaches pace variation. The 3-minute center rep approaches VO2max stimulus.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 8. 5x600m at RP (Bridge Workout)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog
- **Total hard**: 3K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Bridges the gap between 400m reps and 800m reps. Longer than sprints, shorter than true VO2max.
- **When**: Build phase. Good stepping stone workout.

### 9. 3x1mi at TP (Threshold Miles)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: Mile-based threshold work. Easy to pace on roads without a track.
- **When**: Build/peak phase.

### 10. 6x800m at IP (Standard VO2max)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: The standard VO2max session for intermediate runners. More volume than 4x800m.
- **When**: Peak phase. Progress from 4x800m to this.

### 11. 25min Tempo with Strides
- **Structure**: 25min continuous at TP, then 4x20sec strides at mile pace with full recovery
- **Purpose**: Combines threshold stimulus with neuromuscular speed work. Two-for-one session.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 12. Easy Long Run with Pickups
- **Structure**: 6-8mi easy with 6x30sec pickups at 10K effort scattered through the middle miles
- **Purpose**: Adds neuromuscular stimulus without making it a workout. Breaks up the monotony of easy running.
- **When**: Base and build phases.

## Pace Calculation Guidelines for Plan Engine

### From Goal 5K Time
- **Race Pace (RP)**: goal_time / 3.10686 (sec/mi)
- **Interval Pace (IP)**: RP * 0.95 (5% faster than RP — VO2max stimulus)
- **Threshold Pace (TP)**: RP * 1.08 (~8% slower than RP, ~25-30 sec/mi slower)
- **Easy Pace**: RP * 1.50 to RP * 1.70 (50-70% slower than RP)
- **Strides Pace (SP)**: RP * 0.85 (~mile race effort)
- **Long Run Pace**: RP * 1.55 (60-90 sec/mi slower than RP)

### Current Fitness Progression
- Start paces from current fitness (analyzer data), not goal
- Blend toward goal paces as mileage builds (same pattern as marathon plan)
- VO2max intervals should use current IP, progressing toward goal IP
- Threshold should use current TP, progressing toward goal TP

### Pace Rounding
- All paces rounded to nearest 5 seconds
- Interval paces shown as a range (±5sec) for flexibility

## Key Differences from Marathon Training

| Aspect | Marathon | 5K |
|--------|----------|-----|
| Primary limiter | Glycogen depletion, LT | VO2max, speed endurance |
| Key workout | MP long runs | VO2max intervals + race-pace reps |
| Long run emphasis | Race simulation at MP | Aerobic base only (no pace work) |
| Threshold importance | Critical (race is at ~LT) | Important but secondary to VO2max |
| Speed work | Minimal (strides only) | Essential (200-400m reps for economy) |
| Taper length | 2-3 weeks | 7-10 days |
| Mileage ceiling | 60-80+ mpw | 45-65 mpw (diminishing returns beyond) |
| Race-pace in training | MP runs, long run finishes | 1K-mile reps at 5K pace |
| Weekly structure | 2 quality + 1 quality long run | 2 quality + easy long run |
