# Coaching policy decisions

These are deterministic Stride defaults for one athlete, not universal exercise-science rules. See `training-principles.md`, the distance-specific training-science documents and `ARCHITECTURE.md` for the existing training rationale. The exact templates and evidence thresholds below are engineering policy; those documents do not establish that these numbers are optimal for every runner.

## Session controls and approval

Unrun prescribed days can be skipped, replaced or moved to a later unrun easy/rest day in the block. Completed activities are immutable. Every proposed schedule is validated against rest preferences, hard-day spacing and the saved weekly mileage budget; rejected requests roll back completely. Originals and explanations remain available. A move removes the source rather than adding another workout.

The UI generates an inactive persisted preview. Approve atomically activates it; Make it lighter uses the existing recovery-load policy (70% of baseline) without changing training paces. A changed date, goal, athlete preferences, activities, progression or active schedule invalidates approval. Both `/preview` and the legacy `/generate` route now create drafts; only explicit approval activates a block. SQLite schedule writes serialize their read/validate/write transactions.

## Program and phase selection (ladder-v2)

Progression belongs to a versioned distance program and category, rather than a global level. Existing unscoped records belong to the former 5K ladder. A new distance starts at level 1, preserving a demonstrated category pace only when its workout date is within 84 days. Returning to an existing program resumes its own level; workout feedback updates the program that prescribed it. Phase- or budget-reduced sessions can demonstrate pace but cannot advance the full program level. These are conservative transfer rules, not evidence that fitness disappears on day 85.

Base sessions use two shorter threshold repetitions (65% of build work minutes, at least five minutes each) or four short intervals. Build adds duration/repetitions within the program. Peak half/marathon work consolidates threshold minutes into two longer repetitions; short races change interval distance. Taper uses two four-minute threshold repetitions or three short intervals. Work pace is unchanged across these variants. Mileage/general/ultra threshold programs begin with 2×6 minutes, 5K/10K with 2×8, half/marathon with 2×10; each four-level band adds a repetition while intermediate levels extend duration. Existing 5K build templates are preserved. All are subject to the existing budget fitter; level does not override available mileage. Ultra hill and long-run periodization remains in the existing dedicated modules.

The 84-day transfer window follows the existing calibration lookback. Rep counts, durations and phase reductions are Stride heuristics motivated by gradual exposure, specificity and lower taper volume, with no claim of experimentally validated dose equivalence between levels/programs. No goal-time prediction is treated as proof of demonstrated fitness.

## Structured work and evidence (schema version 1)

New rows store a numeric prescription and execution separately. Quality segments have rep counts, meters or seconds, numeric pace bounds, recoveries, program, phase and level. Work display is generated from these fields. Warmup/cooldown and full session distance are included in budget accounting; time-based work is estimated at the faster bound and rounded up to half a mile. Effort-based hills have numeric duration and an explicitly absent pace target; ordinary runs retain their effort/fueling guidance as a note alongside distance. Older rows retain their original text path without being rewritten.

Labeled Garmin work laps carry high confidence. Unlabeled laps use the existing detector (work pace at most 82% of the known easy pace), carry medium confidence, and still must match the prescribed work. Distance tolerance is 8%, duration tolerance 10%, following existing lap detection defaults. Extra/ambiguous work cannot advance. A continuous effort cannot be proved by adding broken efforts together. Mixed workouts must include their speed segment too. These tolerances accommodate GPS and lap-button variation; they are product defaults rather than physiological boundaries. Whole-run averages never advance a level. New structured sessions with only prose hold pending numeric work evidence; old rows still use the legacy evaluator. Hills do not update a flat-pace ladder.

Calibration-v2 treats intervals, continuous tempo, broken threshold and strides independently. It uses one median per workout day, at least two days within 84 days, including one within 28 days. A recency-weighted median (weight `max(0.1, 1 - age/84)`) limits old workouts and large rep counts from dominating. Numeric completed work is preferred and deduplicated by activity; legacy logged splits remain a fallback. No interval-to-race, tempo-to-threshold or tempo-to-steady estimate is reported as calibrated evidence. The 2-day and 28-day requirements and linear weights are cautious engineering defaults, not published scientific cutoffs.

`_build_current_paces` no longer blends an aspirational race goal into threshold/interval pace just because mileage rises from 30 to 60 mpw. Mileage alone does not demonstrate speed. Easy-derived fallback ratios remain 0.77 for threshold and 0.67 for intervals, with the deliberate `tempo = threshold * 0.96` retained and normal five-second rounding. Broken reps remain ten seconds per mile faster than continuous tempo unless independent broken-threshold evidence exists. A five-second work-pace recalibration preserves the template's base/work offset, avoiding an unintended extra ten-second jump. Support-speed/mixed sessions cannot recalibrate an unrelated single pace. These ratios and offsets are existing Stride conventions, not universal training laws.

## Remaining limits

Manual block approval remains required. Automatic post-sync coaching, a UI for correcting uncertain work-lap matching, doubles and a fuller numeric breakdown of mixed long-run/fueling instructions remain future work. Non-quality day guidance is preserved in structured notes; it is not used to prove threshold or interval fitness. Old plans and their prose-based evaluations remain readable. Deployment, multi-user auth, hosted storage and Garmin's official integration are separate work; the application remains intentionally single-athlete.

## October 19 follow-up: variable weeks and block controls

Automatic deloads now require 7 sustained loaded weeks for advanced runners; the previous 3-week threshold is retained for beginners/intermediates. A due deload can start on an untouched Monday, rather than automatically adding another build week. A week at or below 85% of the highest of its previous three calendar weeks counts as a natural lighter week and resets the count. Missing full calendar weeks count as zero. Thus 22 → 33 → 23 → 38 does not trigger a deload. Feedback-based reductions, race tapers and explicit Make it lighter remain separate decisions. These are Stride defaults adjusted for the athlete's preference, not a clinical rule or a claim that deloads should always follow a fixed calendar.

Planning displays the last completed calendar week separately from the mean of the last four full calendar weeks (including empty weeks); a partial current week is counted by the existing budget fitter. A valid saved experience level takes precedence over classification from imported history, since a short import does not make an experienced runner a beginner.

For advanced mileage goals only, a repeatable recent high can anchor the next block: last full week must exceed the four-week average but be at most 135% of it, all four recent calendar weeks must include running (no interruption), and at least two must reach 80% of that recent week. Without a hold/reduce/recovery assessment or a due deload, add at most 5% or 2 miles (whichever is smaller), round to half a mile, and repeat that volume in week two. In the 22/33/23/38 example this yields 40/40, not a 75-mile prescription. This is a repeat-and-observe policy, not a claim that a percentage guarantees safety. A single spike, an interruption, novice/comeback goals, negative block assessment or race taper cannot use this route. Work paces remain unchanged. Existing low-feel feedback still downgrades upcoming work mid-block. On revision, two low-feel days in seven days independently trigger recovery, while a latest low-feel run within three days vetoes the uplift and holds volume; the remaining current week has no prescribed quality workouts. This applies before the five-run-day block assessment minimum so revision cannot erase fatigue evidence.

The 135%, 80%, 5% and 2-mile limits are explicit product heuristics. For context, the [Buist et al. randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/) did not establish that applying a 10% rule prevents running injuries in novices; it does not validate these advanced-runner settings either. The athlete's response remains necessary evidence.

Replacing a workout first redistributes only unrun, unchanged future easy miles, down to the existing 3-mile floor, within that same week. Actual mileage, athlete-modified days, long runs, rest preferences, hard-day spacing and the total budget remain protected. If that is insufficient, the change is rejected and rolled back with an explanation. Scheduled miles and each week's dates are displayed separately rather than as an ambiguous arrow.

An approved active block no longer offers a misleading immediate “next block” action. Current-block revision is an explicit option below the plan and preserves the approved block’s dates, changing only unrun days from today onward. A recorded run today is locked and prescription starts tomorrow. Fully historical weeks retain their actual totals; if the current week is already over the revised target, its completed miles are retained and future days become rest rather than making approval impossible. Very short remaining windows prorate volume and drop an unaffordable quality session. Derived completion status is normalized from real activity evidence in the preview fingerprint, so merely loading the current plan cannot invalidate approval. A true next block is generated only after the current one finishes; it starts in the current training window, never backfills the old block. Keeping the current plan discards only the draft and restores the approved schedule. With no usable approved plan, the review stays available rather than offering a discard into empty content.

## Personal coaching philosophy and editable preferences (October 19, 2026)

The two athlete-supplied ChatGPT coaching reports are preserved in `docs/research/` with provenance, policy mapping and limitations in its README. Mileage goals no longer impose an unconditional one-workout ceiling: `quality_policy.select_quality_policy` permits threshold plus a light speed-support stimulus for advanced runners with recent volume/frequency/long-run evidence and no hold/recovery veto. All numerical gates are initial Stride coaching defaults, not universal science. Low-confidence historical reports are not imported as completed training.

Profile and schedule settings are editable and persisted. Validation checks merged saved preferences even for a partial update. An explicitly empty rest-day list is preserved, including seven-run-day schedules. Updating preferences invalidates existing previews but leaves the approved block unchanged until revision approval. Time format changes bookends and light-session format without changing threshold intensity or measured pace calibration.
