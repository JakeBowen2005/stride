# 10K Training Science & Workout Library

> Research-backed training guide for 10K plan generation.
> Sources: Daniels' Running Formula, Running Writings (percentage-based 10K training),
> Canova specific endurance, Laura Norris Running, Frontiers in Sports (2024).

## Physiological Demands of the 10K

- 10K race effort sits at **90-95% of VO2max** for trained runners
- The 10K is a **threshold event** — raced right around or slightly above lactate threshold
- ~97% aerobic, ~3% anaerobic contribution
- Key limiters: **Lactate threshold** (primary), VO2max (ceiling), running economy, speed endurance
- Unlike 5K (VO2max-limited) or marathon (fuel-limited), the 10K is uniquely **threshold-limited**
- Improving the pace you can sustain at LT directly translates to faster 10K times
- The 10K demands the ability to hold a hard, controlled effort for 30-50 minutes

## How 10K Differs from 5K Training

| Aspect | 5K | 10K |
|--------|-----|------|
| Primary limiter | VO2max | Lactate threshold |
| Race intensity | 95-100% VO2max | 90-95% VO2max |
| Key workout | VO2max intervals | Threshold/tempo + race-pace reps |
| Speed work emphasis | High (200-800m) | Moderate (400-1200m for economy) |
| Threshold emphasis | Secondary | PRIMARY |
| Long run role | Aerobic base only | Aerobic base + some quality finishes |
| Race duration | 15-25 min | 30-55 min |
| Mileage needs | 35-60 mpw | 40-70 mpw |
| Mental demand | Hold on for dear life | Sustained discipline and pacing |

## Key Training Systems for 10K

### 1. Lactate Threshold Development (The #1 Priority)
- **Why**: 10K is raced at or slightly above LT. Raising LT = faster 10K.
- **Traditional threshold**: 20-45 minutes continuous at ~25-30 sec/mi slower than 10K pace
- **Cruise intervals**: 5-15 min reps at threshold with 1-3 min rest
- **Float recovery threshold** (modern approach): Threshold reps with jog recovery that's itself a moderate effort (not full rest). Bridges classical threshold with race-specific work.
- **Double threshold sessions**: AM sub-threshold + PM supra-threshold (advanced only)
- **Critical Velocity (CV)**: Fastest pace for 30-40 min — sits between LT and VO2max. CV training improves both simultaneously.

### 2. Race-Pace Specificity
- **Why**: You need to know what 10K pace feels like and sustain it
- **How**: 10K-pace intervals (1K-2K reps), continuous runs at 10K effort
- **Key principle**: Start with shorter reps at race pace, extend the volume over weeks
- **Progression**: 6x1K → 5x1.5K → 4x2K → 3x3K at 10K pace (over 6-8 weeks)
- **Research**: "8km of work at 100% 10K pace" is the minimum for final peak workouts

### 3. VO2max Support (The Ceiling)
- **Why**: Higher VO2max = more room above LT = faster LT pace possible
- **How**: 800m-1200m reps at 5K pace (VO2max intensity)
- **Less volume needed than 5K training** — 1 session every 1-2 weeks
- **Purpose is supportive, not primary** — maintain/slightly improve VO2max while focusing on threshold

### 4. Endurance at Moderate Intensity
- **Why**: 10K requires sustained effort for 30-50 min. Need to build that durability.
- **10K-supportive endurance (90% of 10K pace)**: 8-10 mile continuous runs
- **10K-specific endurance (95% of 10K pace)**: 7-8 mile continuous runs or alternating K reps
- **Long runs**: 10-14 miles easy, some with steady or progressive finish

### 5. Speed / Running Economy
- **Why**: Better economy = less oxygen cost = can sustain threshold longer
- **How**: Strides 2-3x/week, occasional 400m reps, hill sprints
- **Less emphasis than 5K** — speed exists, need to extend it

## Periodization for 10K (10-16 week cycle)

### Phase 1: General / Base (4-6 weeks)
- Build mileage, easy runs + strides
- Introduce threshold work (1x/week) in second half
- Long runs at easy effort, occasional progression finish
- Optional: Kenyan-style progression runs (unstructured, start very easy, gradually build)
- Fartlek sessions for variety
- Purpose: Aerobic foundation, build volume safely

### Phase 2: Race-Supportive / Build (3-4 weeks)
- Threshold becomes primary quality session (1-2x/week)
- Introduce VO2max intervals (1x every 1-2 weeks)
- 10K-supportive endurance runs at 90% 10K pace
- Long runs can include steady finish (last 2-3mi at steady effort)
- Purpose: Develop LT, begin race-pace introduction

### Phase 3: Race-Specific / Peak (4-6 weeks)
- 10K-pace intervals become primary (1x/week)
- Threshold maintained (1x/week)
- VO2max session every 2 weeks (reduced, supportive role)
- 10K-specific endurance runs at 95% race pace
- Mileage holds or slightly decreases
- Purpose: Sharpen race fitness, accumulate volume at 10K pace

### Phase 4: Taper (10-14 days)
- **Volume reduction: 40-50%** of peak mileage
- **Maintain intensity** — shorter quality sessions
- Reduce easy run distances, keep 1-2 speed sessions
- Final quality session 4-5 days before race
- Last 2 days: easy jog + strides or rest

## Workout Library: Experienced Runners (40+ mpw)

> Paces reference: RP = 10K race pace, TP = threshold pace (~15-20s/mi slower than RP),
> IP = 5K pace / VO2max pace (~15-20s/mi faster than RP), SP = strides/rep pace

### 1. 5x2K at RP (Peak 10K Workout)
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 10K at race pace (broken into 2K chunks)
- **Purpose**: The definitive 10K workout. 10K of volume at exact race pace. If you can do this, you can race it.
- **Progression**: Start with 4x2K, build to 5x2K, then tighten rest from 3min to 2min.
- **When**: Peak phase (final 4-6 weeks).

### 2. 8x1K at RP (Race-Pace Volume)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 8K at race pace
- **Purpose**: High volume of race-pace work with manageable rep length. Builds pace familiarity.
- **When**: Build/peak transition. First race-pace workout in the cycle.

### 3. 3x3K at RP (Extended Race-Pace Reps)
- **Rest**: 3-4min jog
- **Total hard**: 9K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Longer reps force sustained concentration at race effort. Mental toughness builder.
- **When**: Peak phase.

### 4. 3x2mi at TP (Threshold Volume)
- **Rest**: 90sec-2min jog
- **Total hard**: 6mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: The staple threshold workout. Raises LT — the primary limiter for 10K.
- **When**: All phases. Build from 2x2mi to 3x2mi to 4x2mi.

### 5. 6x1200m at TP with Float Recovery (Modern Threshold)
- **Rest**: 400m float at 80-85% 10K pace (NOT full jog rest)
- **Total hard**: ~7K at threshold with active recovery
- **Purpose**: Bridges classical threshold with race-specific endurance. The float recovery keeps HR elevated, simulating race conditions.
- **Progression**: Start with 400m float at easy pace → progress to 80% → then 85% 10K pace.
- **When**: Build/peak phases. Based on Running Writings methodology.

### 6. 6x800m at IP (VO2max Support)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Maintains/improves VO2max ceiling. Supportive session — not the main focus.
- **When**: Build phase. 1x every 1-2 weeks.

### 7. Canova Descending Ladder: 2K + 1600m + 1200m + 800m + 4x400m
- **Rest**: 200m jog between all reps
- **Pacing**: Start at 10K goal pace, increase to 5K pace by 800m, 400s faster
- **Total hard**: ~7.5K of quality with pace progression
- **Purpose**: Race simulation — teaches progressive acceleration. The final 400s simulate the kick.
- **When**: Peak phase.

### 8. 4x(1200m at RP + 800m at IP) — Mixed Speed Session
- **Rest**: 1min between 1200m and 800m, 3min between sets
- **Total hard**: ~8K (4.8K at RP + 3.2K at IP)
- **Purpose**: Alternates race pace with faster-than-race-pace. Teaches holding form when shifting gears.
- **When**: Peak phase. Canova-style mixed speed.

### 9. 10K-Specific Endurance Run: 7-8mi at 95% RP
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: 95% of goal 10K pace — slightly easier than race effort
- **Purpose**: Sustained race-effort pacing. Builds lactate clearance at near-race intensity. The closest simulation to racing without actually racing.
- **When**: Peak phase. 1-2x in the final 4 weeks.

### 10. 10K-Supportive Endurance: 8-10mi at 90% RP
- **Rest**: None (continuous)
- **Pacing**: 90% of goal 10K pace — "strong" effort
- **Purpose**: Builds durability at moderately hard effort. Strengthens aerobic system without the stress of race-pace work.
- **When**: Build phase. Build from 6mi to 8-10mi over several weeks.

### 11. 16x400m at RP with 200m Walk/Jog (Supra-Threshold)
- **Rest**: 200m walk/jog (~60-75sec)
- **Total hard**: 6.4K at race pace
- **Purpose**: Massive race-pace volume with micro-recovery. Lactate stabilizes between reps. Extremely 10K-specific.
- **When**: Peak phase. Based on Running Writings "supra-threshold" protocol.

### 12. Progression Run: 8mi (Easy → Steady → Threshold)
- **Structure**: 3mi easy, 3mi at steady, 2mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: Progressive effort teaches pacing discipline. Finishing at threshold on tired legs mimics late-race demands.
- **When**: Build phase. Every 2-3 weeks.

## Workout Library: Beginner/Intermediate Runners (20-40 mpw)

> Focus: Build aerobic base, develop threshold, introduce race-pace work gradually.
> Beginners: 1 quality session per week for first 4 weeks, then 2.

### 1. 2x10min at TP (Intro Threshold)
- **Rest**: 3min jog
- **Total hard**: 20min at threshold
- **Purpose**: Introduction to sustained hard effort. Simple and effective.
- **When**: Build phase. The first quality workout for 10K training.

### 2. 4x800m at IP (Intro VO2max)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 2mi at VO2max pace
- **Purpose**: Introduction to fast running. Develops VO2max in small, manageable doses.
- **When**: Build phase. Generous rest allows quality.

### 3. 20min Continuous Tempo
- **Rest**: None
- **Total hard**: 20min at threshold
- **Purpose**: Builds sustained effort capacity. The bread-and-butter beginner threshold workout.
- **When**: Build phase. Progress from 15min to 20min to 25min.

### 4. 4x1K at RP (Race Pace Introduction)
- **Rest**: 2-3min jog
- **Total hard**: 4K at 10K pace
- **Purpose**: Learn what 10K race pace feels like. Short enough reps to maintain quality.
- **When**: Build/peak phase.

### 5. Fartlek: 6x2min Hard / 2min Easy
- **Rest**: Equal time easy jog
- **Pacing**: Hard segments at 10K effort (not pace — effort)
- **Total hard**: 12min at quality effort
- **Purpose**: Unstructured speed work. Lower pressure than track intervals. Good for building comfort with hard running.
- **When**: Base/build phase.

### 6. Fartlek Pyramid: 1-2-3-4-3-2-1 min Hard / 1min Easy
- **Rest**: 1min easy jog between all segments
- **Pacing**: Effort-based — "comfortably hard" to "hard"
- **Total hard**: 16min of quality
- **Purpose**: Teaches pace variation and sustained effort. The 3-4 minute center reps approach VO2max.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 7. 3x1mi at TP (Threshold Miles)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 3mi at threshold
- **Purpose**: Mile-based threshold. Easy to pace on roads. Simple structure.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 8. 6x600m at RP (Bridge Workout)
- **Rest**: 90sec jog
- **Total hard**: ~2.3mi at race pace
- **Purpose**: Longer than 400m sprints, shorter than 1K reps. Transition workout for building toward longer race-pace reps.
- **When**: Build phase.

### 9. 25min Tempo + Strides
- **Structure**: 25min continuous at TP, then 4x20sec strides at mile effort with full recovery
- **Purpose**: Threshold stimulus with neuromuscular speed kicker. Two-for-one session.
- **When**: Build/peak phase.

### 10. 5x1K at RP (Standard Race-Pace Session)
- **Rest**: 2min jog
- **Total hard**: 5K at 10K pace
- **Purpose**: The standard race-pace workout for intermediate 10K runners. Enough volume to be meaningful, short enough reps to maintain pace.
- **When**: Peak phase. Progress from 4x1K.

### 11. Easy Long Run with Steady Finish
- **Structure**: 8-10mi total. First 6-7mi easy, last 2-3mi at steady effort (not threshold — just picking it up)
- **Purpose**: Aerobic base with a quality finish. Teaches running strong when tired without the stress of a full workout.
- **When**: Build phase. Every other long run.

### 12. 3x(5min Hard + 5min Easy) Continuous Fartlek
- **Rest**: No stopping — easy segments are active recovery
- **Total hard**: 15min at threshold-to-10K effort
- **Purpose**: Builds ability to sustain and recover from hard efforts repeatedly. Simulates the surge-and-settle pattern of 10K racing.
- **When**: Build phase. Great for runners who don't have track access.

## Pace Calculation Guidelines for Plan Engine

### From Goal 10K Time
- **Race Pace (RP)**: goal_time / 6.21371 (sec/mi)
- **Interval Pace (IP)**: RP * 0.93 (7% faster — approximates 5K pace / VO2max)
- **Threshold Pace (TP)**: RP * 1.05 (5% slower — lactate threshold)
- **Easy Pace**: RP * 1.40 to RP * 1.60 (40-60% slower)
- **Steady Pace**: RP * 1.15 to RP * 1.20 (within a minute of RP)
- **Strides Pace (SP)**: RP * 0.82 (~mile race effort)
- **Long Run Pace**: RP * 1.45 (easy, conversational)
- **10K-Supportive (90% RP)**: RP * 1.11 (continuous endurance runs)
- **10K-Specific (95% RP)**: RP * 1.05 (near-race continuous runs)

### Percentage-Based Pace System (Running Writings Method)
| % of 10K Pace | Use |
|----------------|-----|
| 110% | VO2max / 5K pace (supportive speed) |
| 105% | 5K pace (specific speed) |
| 100% | 10K race pace (primary intervals) |
| 95% | 10K-specific endurance (continuous runs) |
| 90% | 10K-supportive endurance (long tempo runs) |
| 85-90% | Strong / steady runs |
| 70-80% | Moderate effort |
| 55-65% | Easy running |
| 45-55% | Recovery running |

### Current Fitness Progression
- Same blending pattern as marathon/5K plans
- Start paces from current fitness, blend toward goal as mileage builds
- Threshold progression is more important for 10K than for 5K
- Race-pace work introduced later in cycle than 5K (needs bigger aerobic base first)

### Pace Rounding
- All paces rounded to nearest 5 seconds
- Interval paces shown as range (±5sec) for flexibility

## Special 10K Training Concepts

### Float Recovery (Key Differentiator)
Traditional: Hard rep → easy jog → hard rep
Float: Hard rep → moderate jog (80-85% RP) → hard rep

Float recovery keeps HR elevated between reps, preventing full recovery. This simulates race conditions where you never fully recover. Critical for 10K training.

**Progression over weeks:**
1. Threshold reps with easy jog recovery
2. Same reps with float at easy-to-moderate effort
3. Same reps with float at 80% 10K pace
4. Same reps with float at 85% 10K pace
5. Continuous alternation: 1K at threshold / 1K at 80-85%

### Kenyan-Style Progression Runs
- Start extremely easy (much slower than normal easy pace)
- Gradually accelerate through middle miles to strong aerobic pace
- Optional: finish final 1-2 miles faster than steady
- Unstructured — run by feel, not splits
- Purpose: Builds aerobic endurance with natural pace exploration
- **When**: General/build phases, 1x every 2 weeks

### Racing as Training
- **5K races** count as "10K-specific speed" sessions
- **10K races** count as "10K-specific endurance" sessions
- **15K/10mi races** count as "10K-supportive endurance"
- Can substitute for equivalent quality workouts in the plan

### The 80% Rule
Expect to complete only ~80% of prescribed workouts due to illness, scheduling, or unexpected performance fluctuations. The plan engine should not penalize missed workouts — just adjust and keep moving forward.
